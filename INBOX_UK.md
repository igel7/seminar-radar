# INBOX_UK — 見つけたセミナーを放り込む場所(英国モード用)

**英国モード**(英国・アイルランド・北欧。`--profile uk`)の自動巡回で拾えないイベントを見つけたら、ここに貼るだけでよい。ドイツモード(ドイツ・中東欧)のイベントは `INBOX.md` へ。
このファイルは英国モードの日次更新 Routine が処理する(ドイツモードの Routine は読まない)。
スマホのGitHubアプリ・ブラウザどちらでも、鉛筆アイコン(編集)から30秒で終わる。

## 使い方

1. このファイルの下にある区切り線より**下**に貼り付ける:
   - **イベントページのURLを1行貼る**(複数行OK)。翌日の自動更新がURLを開いて
     日付・場所などを抽出してくれる
   - **ブロックされていて自動で開けないサイト**の場合は、
     URLの代わりに、自分のブラウザで開いたページの本文
     (タイトル・日付・場所が含まれる部分)をコピペする
2. 「Commit changes」を押す(mainに直接コミットでOK)

- 取り込まれると、区切り線より下は自動で空に戻る
- すぐ反映したいときは英国モードの日次更新を手動実行する
  (claude.ai/code/routines で英国モードのRoutineの「Run now」)
- 既にカレンダーにあるイベントと重複しても問題ない(自動で統合される)
- 開催日が確認できないものは取り込まれない(日付を捏造しない方針のため)

The energy transition’s new risk landscape
Over the past year, climate and geopolitical shocks have increasingly arrived not as separate crises, but in tandem, exposing the interconnected vulnerabilities of energy systems, economies and societies. As temperatures edge beyond 1.5°C of warming, the international community has seen escalating climate extremes, with record-breaking heat, wildfires and severe floods affecting communities globally.

At the same time, geopolitical fragmentation has persisted:

Ongoing disruption to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz has contributed to   elevated energy and fertiliser prices and renewed concerns over the vulnerability of global supply chains.
Attacks on critical infrastructure in Ukraine have continued to underscore the need for the transition to electrification and renewables.
Yet, both the energy transition and broader climate mitigation efforts bring their own challenges. As countries race to decarbonize, new geopolitical pressures are emerging as nations compete for dominance in clean tech manufacturing, access to critical minerals, control of key supply chains and industrial leadership in the low-carbon economy. The chapter of unanimous climate negotiations and target-setting is giving way to a new era defined by the implementation of strategies in an age of disruption. Building resilience must now go hand in hand with accelerating the transition and tackling pressing climate change concerns.

During two days of high-level keynotes, panel dialogues, networking sessions, and spotlight sessions held under the Chatham House Rule, speakers and delegates will examine how governments, businesses and societies can manage mounting risks while maintaining energy security, economic prosperity and international cooperation towards the transition. What does climate and energy resilience mean in an era of intensifying shocks? And crucially, what does it mean for business?

Agenda
Wednesday 10 March
When supply chains break: How Hormuz reshaped the world’s transition
One year on from the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, this session draws on global case studies to examine its implications for energy transitions around the world.

How did the closure of the Strait of Hormuz reshape national energy security strategies and alter the trajectory of energy transitions around the world?
How has the crisis rearranged geopolitical alliances and brought states into competition over critical supply chains?
What factors have so far determined success in accelerating efforts at making the energy
transition toward cleaner sources of power, and how have countries embraced these strategies as part of energy security strategies?
How have regions that rely heavily on oil and gas imports regions responded?
One year on, is the world better prepared for the next major energy shock, and what vulnerabilities remain? 
0900-1000
The new geopolitics of green industry
This session explores how industrial policy, strategic investment and international competition are reshaping the global energy transition.

How can governments use policy and public finance to mobilize private capital and accelerate industrial decarbonization while strengthening competitiveness and energy security?
What lessons can be taken from different countries’ approaches to the green industrial transition, including China’s green tech industry development over the past 10-15 years and the current role of Japan’s GX Agency in promoting industrial transition and providing transition finance?
How are governments seeking to attract investment into strategic low-carbon industries such as green steel, sustainable aviation fuel, low-carbon cement and clean shipping, and which approaches are proving most effective?
As the global race for clean technology value chains intensifies, how can countries most effectively capture economic value from emerging clean electricity and industrial supply chains?
What role can international cooperation, particularly among middle economic powers, play in more effectively ‘greening’ industrial strategy?
1000-1100
Networking break
1100-1130
What does a resilient energy system of the future look like?
In this session, panellists explore a vision for an energy system of the future that can both accelerate the transition and remain resilient in the face of the unknowns of tomorrow’s geopolitical landscape.

How does electrification contribute to energy system resilience and which regions have provided strong case studies?
What does the optimal resilient energy system of 2050 look like?
Should countries prioritize energy sovereignty, or does true resilience depend on greater international integration?
To what extent can the transition to electrification and renewables foster resilience against physical, cyber and geopolitical threats, and what new vulnerabilities might it open up?
How should energy systems of the future be designed to withstand physical and cyber disruption, whether from climate extremes, conflict or attacks on critical infrastructure? 
1130-1230
Keynote session
1230-1300
Networking lunch
1300-1400
Watt’s the cost? A roadmap to affordable electricity
Affordable electricity is critical to maintaining support for the energy transition, but bringing costs down remains a major challenge. In this session, panellists draw up a roadmap to affordable electricity.

Have grids become the weak link in the transition to affordable electricity?
What role can storage, flexibility and demand management play in reducing electricity costs and maximizing the value of renewable power? Can these approaches also deliver a lasting energy security dividend?

Adjusting to the geopolitical reality
The security and defence landscape of 2027 is going through a fundamental readjustment. Governments, armed forces and industry leaders are responding to a strategic environment shaped by increased defence spending, shifting alliances and growing uncertainty about the future balance of power. Long-held assumptions regarding deterrence, burden-sharing and international cooperation are being tested by emerging threats and mounting geopolitical competition, forcing policymakers and private sector stakeholders alike to reconsider how security is delivered and sustained in an increasingly contested world.

As nation states assume greater responsibility for their own security, questions about the future shape of international security have become increasingly urgent:

The global drive toward higher defence spending has strengthened military potential but has also exposed differing national priorities, industrial capacities and political ambitions in the drive for efficient and effective spending
The rapid pace of technological change has the potential to reshape the newer battlegrounds – namely the cyber, AI and space domains of conflict, unleashing new threats and opportunities for stakeholders across the globe.
Alliance structures are being pushed to the limits by national political instincts, which have the potential to upend the norms that have governed this environment for decades. However, amidst this environment is the opportunity for new poles to emerge, innovative solutions to develop and a more holistic analysis of the security landscape to form.
The 2027 Security and Defence Conference will bring together policymakers, senior military leaders, business decisionmakers and civil society experts to examine the defining security challenges of the coming decade. Through a programme of keynote addresses, high-level panel discussions and Chatham House Rule spotlight sessions, participants will advance their understanding of the future of international alliances, defence investment, civil resilience, emerging technologies, space security and the evolving character of conflict. Together, speakers and delegates will identify how governments, armed forces and industry can strengthen partnerships, build resilience and develop the capabilities required to navigate an era of strategic uncertainty.
